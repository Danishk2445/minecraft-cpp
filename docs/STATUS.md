# Status

The plan is `~/.claude/plans/make-minecraft-in-c-sparkling-ladybug.md`, covering the core game in milestones M0–M13. Each milestone ends runnable with its tests passing, and gets a local commit.

## Deviations from vanilla
Recorded per milestone.
- **M1:**
  - Random model variants use the 1.21.5+ rule: `JavaRandom(Mth.getSeed(pos)).nextInt(totalWeight)`. Multipart parts restart from one derived seed. Older versions used `abs((int)nextLong()) % total`.
  - Atlas UVs are pulled 0.1% toward the opposite corner, as in pre-1.21 FaceBakery.
  - All block-atlas sprites are aligned to 16 texels, so the atlas keeps 4 mip levels. The 8×8 conduit eyes would otherwise cap it at 3.
  - `paletted_permutations` atlas sources (armor trims) are skipped.
- **M2:**
  - Block states come from behavior-class schemas (`data/blocks/classes.json`, with schemas in `BlockRegistry.cpp`), or are inferred from blockstate files for blocks without behavior. That gives 25,986 states; vanilla has about 27k, because inferred blocks lack non-visual properties such as note block instruments.
  - Face culling uses a 4×4 coverage grid per face, built from fully opaque flush quads, instead of vanilla's VoxelShape occlusion test.
  - Outline and collision shapes are deferred to M6, where they are first used.
  - Models are drawn without vanilla's random per-position offsets (the XZ, sometimes Y, nudge of flowers, grass, ferns, bamboo, roots and pointed dripstone); planned for M13.
- **M3:**
  - Quads are split along the diagonal joining their brighter corners (anisotropy fix, as in Sodium). Vanilla always splits 0-2.
  - Partial quads interpolate the four AO corner values bilinearly. This is equivalent to vanilla's `SizeInfo` weight tables.
  - The lightmap is sampled at texel centers, so integer light levels are exact. Vanilla samples at uv/256, which blends neighboring levels.
  - Fog: render-distance fog covers the last `clamp(rd/10, 4, 64)` blocks; environmental fog appears only in rain. Vanilla's exact atmospheric fog constants are not reproduced.
  - Clouds are rebuilt every frame within 16 cells (192 blocks), at a height of 192.33. They fade with distance.
  - The incremental light removal ignores face occlusion: it may over-remove, and re-adding repairs it. A test checks it against from-scratch light after 10,000 random edits.
- **M4:**
  - Noise, splines and density follow vanilla's algorithms (ImprovedNoise, PerlinNoise, NormalNoise, BlendedNoise, CubicSpline, the TerrainProvider splines and the overworld noise router). Every noise is seeded from our own Xoroshiro name hashing, so worlds are not seed-compatible with vanilla.
  - Noise caves have no `pillars` term. Only the sign of the final density is used, so the 0.64 multiplier and `squeeze` are skipped.
  - Aquifers are a stand-in for vanilla's noise-based aquifer grid. Below sea level, air turns to water down to a flood line 6 blocks under the spline surface; dry air in the 3 blocks under that line becomes stone (a barrier); deeper caves stay dry, and lava fills air below y −54. There are no randomly flooded deep aquifers.
  - Carvers have vanilla's structure and parameters: `cave` (15%), `cave_extra_underground` (7%, y −56..47) and `canyon` (1%), drawn from our own random streams. Instead of consulting an aquifer, carvers never open a block that touches water.
  - Surface biomes come from threshold tables equivalent to `OverworldBiomeBuilder`'s parameter list. Cave biomes use vanilla's nearest-point rule against the surface biomes' depth.
  - Surface rules are a hand-written approximation of vanilla's rule tree: grass/dirt, sand/sandstone, ocean gravel, river beds, snowy peaks and slopes, gravelly hills, badlands terracotta bands, calcite, podzol, coarse dirt, mycelium and swamp clay.
  - Spawn: the nearest land column on a square spiral of 16-block steps that is not ocean, river, beach or peaks. Vanilla searches climate parameter space instead. The free camera starts 12 blocks above it until the player arrives in M6.
  - The biome statistics test samples 32768² blocks per seed, not 4096². The continentalness noise varies over about 2,000 blocks, so 4096² samples range from 16% to 53% ocean. Over large areas ocean is 29–31%.
  - Fluid side faces are hidden only by a neighbor whose face is fully covered. Vanilla also compares the fluid's height with the neighbor's shape.
- **M5:**
  - Feature data (`data/worldgen/features/*.json`, `biome_features.json`) reproduces vanilla's configured and placed features from memory; there are no vanilla data files. The 1.21.5 additions (bushes, dry grass, leaf litter, wildflowers, firefly bushes, fallen trees, cactus flowers) are the least certain.
  - Features decide against the TERRAIN snapshots of their 3×3 area plus their own source's writes, so features from neighboring source chunks cannot see each other. MERGE applies writes feature by feature and skips stale ones; a post-pass removes column-supported blocks (plants, dripstone, carpets) whose support another source replaced. Overlapping trees from different sources can keep leaf distances that are too large (about 3% of leaves); leaf decay arrives in M11.
  - Not generated: structures (monster rooms, geodes, fossils, desert wells), mangrove swamps, pale gardens and the deep dark (as planned). Bee nests have no bees. Springs stay single source blocks until fluids tick (M11).
  - Underwater magma almost never appears: it needs flooded caves at least two blocks below the sea floor, which the aquifer stand-in rarely makes.
  - Noise-driven counts and states (flower patches, coral, kelp, bamboo, meadow flowers) use stand-in noises, and geometry uses `std::sin`/`std::cos` rather than vanilla's lookup tables. Worlds are not seed-compatible anyway.
  - Ice spikes and groves get snow-block surfaces (vanilla mixes in powder snow). Snow and ice ignore block light, which does not exist yet at generation time. Mushrooms survive regardless of light during generation.
  - Every top-level random call is sequenced: C++ leaves the order of operands such as `nextInt() - nextInt()` unspecified, and worlds must not depend on the compiler.
- **M6:**
  - Shapes: outlines and collision come from model element boxes (element rotations ignored), with vanilla overrides for fences, panes, walls, gates, snow layers, cactus, soul sand, mud, honey, lily pads, ladders, torches and the code-rendered blocks (chests, beds, signs, banners, heads, shulker boxes, pot, conduit). Flat models (flowers, vines) outline their quads' bounds. Vanilla's hand-written shapes differ in detail (for example flower outlines are smaller).
  - Step-up uses the pre-1.21 "step up, then across" search; 1.21's candidate step heights give the same result for blocks.
  - Placement covers the vanilla rules for stairs, slabs (with doubling), doors (approximate hinge choice), beds, double plants, trapdoors, buttons, levers, grindstones, torches, signs, banners, heads, ladders, rails (straight), chests (pairing), hoppers, pistons, observers, dispensers, barrels, anvils, lanterns, rods and pillars; other blocks fall back to their `facing`/`axis`/`rotation` properties. Wall `tall` sides follow only whether the block above has a sturdy bottom face.
  - Saves keep only edited chunks: every chunk still generates (so neighbors receive its tree and plant writes), and a saved chunk's blocks replace the generated ones before lighting. Light is not saved. `/assertblock` stands in for the planned `--dump-world-state` diff.
  - Particles: block break and mining debris only (vanilla TerrainParticle); other particle types come with the simulation milestone.
  - The hotbar shows slot counts and the selected item's name; item icons arrive with the GUI milestone.
- **M7:**
  - Block entities are baked into the chunk meshes as static models.
    - A chest or ender chest whose lid is moving (M10) leaves the mesh and draws as an entity until it shuts. Banners don't wave, and the bell doesn't swing.
    - They take the flat light of their cell; vanilla lights each block entity at its own position, so the result is the same except for smooth lighting.
    - Their textures live in the block atlas, so they get mipmaps; vanilla's entity atlases have none.
  - Banners show their base color only, as planned (no patterns). Decorated pots have no sherds. Sign boards are part of the mesh at any distance; their text draws as an entity within 64 blocks (M10), where vanilla draws the whole sign.
  - Not drawn: the books of enchanting tables and lecterns, beacon beams, and the contents of spawners, vaults and campfires.
  - The player is always Steve with wide arms. Vanilla picks one of nine default skins from the player's UUID.
  - Items in hand are not drawn yet: third-person held items and the first-person hand come with item rendering (M8).
  - Spectators see their own head at 15% opacity in third person, with the body hidden, as in vanilla.
  - The harness's `--hold forward,attack,...` holds controls down for a whole run. It drives the walking, mining and sneaking screenshots.
- **M8:**
  - Unifont is not in the pack, so characters the bitmap fonts lack draw vanilla's missing box.
  - GUI item icons are drawn one by one with their own depth slices. Vanilla 1.21.6+ renders them into an item atlas first; they look the same.
  - Item definitions are fully parsed, but items carry no data components yet. Properties that read them take their defaults: damage, dyes, potions, trims, bundles, block state and charge type. Clocks follow the time of day, compasses point at the world spawn (and spin without a holder), and chests switch to the Christmas texture from 24 to 26 December.
  - Equipment slots (which armor slot an item fits) and rarities (name colors) come from rules in `data/items/items.json`.
  - Creative inventory: tab contents are vanilla's order as written from memory (`data/creative_tabs.json`). There is no Saved Hotbars tab, and the Operator Utilities tab stays hidden. Search matches names and ids, not tags or tooltips.
  - There is no recipe book (out of scope).
  - First-person hands have no view bobbing (M13).
- **M9:**
  - The pack's panorama is six 1×1 placeholders, so the game bakes its own into `<gamedir>/panorama` on first start (a few seconds): our seed 42 from (−60, 79, −250) at time 2500. `--bake-panorama` re-bakes it; with `--seed`, `--pos`, `--look` and `--time` it bakes another view. The pack's `panorama_overlay` is transparent, so nothing tints it.
  - Title screen: no fade-in. The version line names this game, and the corner where vanilla shows its copyright line reads "Not an official Minecraft product." Multiplayer, Realms, Language (en_us only) and Accessibility are disabled.
  - World list: Edit World and Re-Create are disabled. There are no lock, version or backup warnings, and every world shows version 1.21.8. Deleting the last world leaves an empty list; vanilla moves on to Create World.
  - Create World: Generate Structures and Bonus Chest are fixed off, since this game has neither. Customize (superflat presets), Game Rules, Experiments and Data Packs are disabled. World types are Default, Superflat and Debug Mode, all in the cycle; vanilla shows Debug Mode only with Shift and also has Large Biomes, Amplified and Single Biome. Hardcore worlds are saved as hardcore; their death screen offers only Spectate World (M10).
  - Loading screen: the map shows our pipeline stages in vanilla's ChunkStatus colors, over the render-distance square instead of vanilla's spawn chunk radius.
  - "Saving world" is shown for a single frame, because saving blocks the main thread.
  - Commands use vanilla's syntax, but without target selectors (they act on the player), tab completion or translated feedback. `/gamerule` knows doDaylightCycle, and since M10 keepInventory and naturalRegeneration; the other rules arrive with their systems. `/clear` takes no target. The 16 commands: help, list, me, seed, say, difficulty, gamemode, give, clear, tp/teleport, time, gamerule, setblock, fill and the harness's assertblock.
  - Options are saved as `options.json`, not vanilla's `options.txt`. Particles has no effect yet: the only particles are block breaking and mining, which vanilla shows at every setting. View Bobbing arrives with M13. Menu Background Blur sits in Video Settings (vanilla also has it under Accessibility).
- **M10:**
  - Data: tool tiers, armor materials, foods, fuel times, item tags (`data/items/items.json`, `data/tags/items`), block loot (`data/loot/blocks.json`) and recipes (`data/recipes`) are written from vanilla 1.21.8 by hand. Loot uses a compact rule format (an item's own drop by default; counts uniform, binomial or from a property; `else` for a failed chance; `player_only`) instead of loot-table JSON.
  - No enchantments, so no Silk Touch, Fortune, Mending or Unbreaking: ores always drop their raw items, glass and ice drop nothing, and so on.
  - Recipes: 1,063 load, with mirrored shaped matching, shapeless matching and the tool repair recipe. Missing are the special recipes that need data components: armor dyeing, banner copying and patterns, book and map cloning, firework stars, suspicious stew and tipped arrows. Firework rockets have one fixed recipe, and a decorated pot is made from four bricks. Stonecutting and smithing have no menus. Recipes are never locked.
  - Damage: falling (the 1.21.5 order, including the landing tick's movement), drowning, suffocation, lava, fire and burning, cactus, sweet berry bushes, magma (unless sneaking), the void, starvation and `/kill`. Mobs, arrows, explosions and lightning come with M11 and M12, along with difficulty damage scaling, which applies only to mobs. There is no freezing (no powder snow) and there are no status effects.
  - Death drops the inventory (unless keepInventory) and min(7·level, 100) experience. Death messages cover the sources above. Respawning goes to the bed if it is still there, else to the world spawn with vanilla's "missing or obstructed" message. Respawn anchors are out of scope.
  - Beds: sleep at night (sky darkening ≥ 4), skip to the next morning after 100 ticks, and set the spawn. Thunderstorms (M11) and the "monsters nearby" rule (M12) come later. Getting up uses a simplified `BedBlock.findStandUpPosition`: the first free spot on solid ground around the head, then around the foot, else on top of the bed. Vanilla tries the sides in a fixed order and also looks one block up and down.
  - Containers: single and double chests, trapped chests (no redstone signal), barrels, ender chests (the player's own 27 slots, with the lid), and furnaces, smokers and blast furnaces. An opaque block over a chest keeps it shut, as in vanilla; cats can't sit on chests. There are no container sounds, and furnaces keep no record of recipes used.
  - Signs: front and back text, dyes, glow ink, ink sacs and wax, as in vanilla. There are no click events and no text filtering. `/setblock` takes vanilla's sign NBT (`front_text`, `back_text`, `is_waxed`), and the save uses the same names. Glowing text sits 0.1 text pixels in front of its outline, where vanilla uses a polygon offset.
  - Item use: hoes, shovels, axes (strip, scrape, unwax), shears on pumpkins, honeycomb on copper and signs, flint and steel (fire, campfires, candles), eating and drinking with vanilla's consume times and crumbs, cake, and right-clicking armor on. Milk and potions are drunk but do nothing. Buckets and bone meal come with M11, bows with M12.
  - Attack strength recovers as in vanilla and resets on a swing at nothing or a change of item; a miss outside creative pauses attacking for 10 ticks. The crosshair or hotbar indicator shows it (and, since M12, the full sprite over a living target).
  - HUD: hearts blink for 20 (healing: 10) ticks when health changes during invulnerability, and the lost health shows until it has held for 20 ticks, where vanilla waits one second of real time. Hardcore worlds use the hardcore hearts. There are no absorption, poison, wither or frozen hearts (no effects).
  - The red hurt tint and the dying fall-over draw on the player model. Sleeping players lie in their bed in third person. Worn armor is drawn since M12.
  - Item entities and experience orbs follow vanilla's physics, merging and pickup, and are saved with their chunk (item, age, pickup delay and health; orb value, count and age).
- **M11:**
  - The simulation (`src/world/sim`) is vanilla's block behavior keyed by behavior class and id, not per-block classes. `Level::setBlockAndUpdate` runs the new block's `onPlace`, its neighbors' `neighborChanged` (vanilla's order W, E, D, U, N, S) and the shape updates (up to 512) at once and recursively. Vanilla queues neighbor updates and runs them after the current one, so long chains can resolve in a different order. Sponges guard against their own nested updates, which vanilla's queue gives for free.
  - Fluids follow `FlowingFluid`: new levels from the neighbors, two sources making a third (water only, by the gamerules), the search for a way down (4 blocks for water, 2 for lava), drop-off, delays (water 5, lava 30 ticks), lava meeting water, currents, buckets and waterlogging. Whether fluid passes between two blocks compares their collision shapes on a 16×16 raster of the shared face, instead of vanilla's occlusion shapes. Fish, axolotl, tadpole and powder snow buckets are out of scope.
  - Random ticks pick 3 (`randomTickSpeed`) blocks per section of every ticking chunk with vanilla's LCG. Vanilla only ticks chunks within 128 blocks of a player; here every chunk with all 8 neighbors lit ticks.
  - Falling blocks: sand, red sand, gravel, concrete powder (sets in or beside water), anvils (hurt the player under them, 2 per block fallen, at most 40, and chip) and the dragon egg (it never teleports). Suspicious sand and gravel break on landing without loot, as in vanilla. Pointed dripstone never falls, and falling blocks carry no block-entity data.
  - Growth: crops, stems and their fruit, saplings (vanilla's tree growers from `data/worldgen/features`, with 2×2 big trees and the bee-nest variants near flowers), sugar cane, cactus (with 1.21.5 flowers), kelp and cave vines, bamboo, vines, sweet berries, cocoa, mushrooms spreading, grass and mycelium spreading, and torchflower crops. Pitcher crops, small and big dripleaf, glow lichen, sea pickles and pointed dripstone don't grow; pale oak saplings have no tree (pale gardens are out); nether wart grows anywhere it stands. Bee nests stay empty.
  - Bone meal works on crops, stems, saplings, grass blocks (short grass and the biome's flowers), short grass and ferns, tall flowers, flower beds, kelp, seagrass, cave vines, bamboo, sweet berries, cocoa, mushrooms (huge mushrooms), azaleas (azalea trees), moss, rooted dirt, bushes and dry grass, and in water (seagrass, corals in warm oceans). It has no effect on small and big dripleaf, glow lichen, sea pickles or pitcher crops.
  - Leaves keep their distance to a log through scheduled ticks and decay by random ticks, as in vanilla; leaves placed by the player are persistent. Grass dies under blocks whose face toward it blocks light or which absorb all of it, an approximation of vanilla's `getLightBlockInto`.
  - Farmland dries out and turns to dirt (unless a crop keeps it), is wetted by water within 4 blocks or by rain, and is trampled by the player's falls (mobs come with M12). Ice melts in block light above 10 and snow layers above 11. Water freezes and snow piles up (to `snowAccumulationHeight` layers) at vanilla's rate of 1 in 48 per random tick per chunk; cauldrons catch rain and snow. Unwaxed copper weathers with vanilla's neighbor rule; budding amethyst grows buds. Wet sponges never dry (no Nether).
  - Fire: vanilla's ignite and burn odds (`ignite_odds`, `burn_odds` in `data/blocks/properties.json`), aging, burnout, spread up to 4 above, rain and the infiniburn blocks (netherrack, magma). Lava sets fire to blocks with ignite odds; vanilla uses a separate "ignited by lava" property (it also lights wooden doors, signs and the like). The biomes that burn out faster are a fixed list.
  - Explosions follow `ServerExplosion`: 1,352 rays against blast resistance (`resistance` in `properties.json`, else the hardness), loot merged into stacks of up to 16 with no drop decay (as for TNT since 1.21), damage and knockback by distance and the seen fraction, items and orbs destroyed. TNT is lit by fire, flint and steel, other explosions (on a short fuse) and `/summon`; redstone and fire arrows can't light it. There are no creepers yet (M12), no fire explosions and no explosion sounds.
  - Weather: vanilla's clear, rain and thunder timers and fading, `/weather`, rain and snow by biome temperature, darker skies and fog, sleeping through thunderstorms (which resets the weather), and lightning: 1 in 100,000 chunk ticks in a thunderstorm, aimed at the player if they stand in the open within 3 blocks of the column. Lightning lights fires on Normal and Hard, hurts (5) and sets alight what it hits, and flashes the sky. There are no lightning rods, skeleton traps or copper scraping, and no visual-only bolts.
  - Particles: vanilla's particle classes for terrain debris, smoke, large smoke, flames (small and soul), lava pops, rain splashes, water splashes, bubbles, underwater motes, water and lava drips (hanging, falling, landing), explosions and their emitter, bone-meal sparkles, tinted and cherry falling leaves, and campfire smoke. Display ticks cover torches, fire, lit furnaces, smokers, blast furnaces and candles, lava, water, drips under fluids, leaves and wet sponges, plus rain splashes and smoke on lava. Campfires don't smoke (their smoke comes from block-entity ticks), and there are no redstone, portal, spore, mycelium, dripstone or biome ambient particles. The Particles option still shows all of them. Particle sprites share the block atlas, so they are mipmapped.
  - Rain and snow draw as vanilla's columns within 10 blocks (the fancy radius), with splashes; there are no rain sounds. Under water and in lava the fog uses vanilla's distances and colors, including the eyes adjusting under water; the water fog color doesn't ease between biomes. There is no powder snow fog and no underwater or fire overlay yet (M13).
  - Flat worlds are plains (biome ids are sorted, so the default id 0 was badlands, which has no rain).
  - Game rules added: doFireTick, waterSourceConversion, lavaSourceConversion, doTileDrops, doEntityDrops, doVinesSpread, doWeatherCycle, randomTickSpeed and snowAccumulationHeight. Commands: `/weather` is new, and `/summon` also takes tnt (with `fuse`) and lightning_bolt. `--weather` starts the harness in fixed weather, and `--hold` now starts with the harness, after `--exec`.
- **M12:**
  - Mobs: pig, cow, sheep, chicken, zombie, skeleton, creeper and spider, with vanilla's sizes, attributes, goals and priorities (`data/entities.json` for the data, the classes in `src/entity/mob`). Their AI is vanilla's goal selector (flags, priorities, every-other-tick goals), move, look, jump and body-rotation controls, ground navigation and `WalkNodeEvaluator` A* path finding (steps, drops by the fall limit, diagonals, doors, fences, water, danger costs). Paths are not recomputed when a block on them changes (vanilla's `shouldRecomputePath`); the goals re-plan every few ticks anyway.
  - Left out from the vanilla mobs: zombie reinforcements, door breaking on Hard, item pickup, drowned and husk conversion, chicken and spider jockeys, the baby zombie's chicken; skeletons fleeing wolves and turning into strays; creepers fleeing cats, fire charges, lingering clouds and the heads charged creepers make drop; spiders' Hard-difficulty effects; pigs struck by lightning; jeb_ sheep; eggs hatching chicks (thrown eggs are out). Entity cramming (`maxEntityCramming`) is not checked.
  - Variants: pigs and chickens are temperate or warm by biome (vanilla's 1.21.5 tags); cold pigs and chickens and every cow but the temperate one need other geometry and stay temperate (as planned). Warm chickens lay brown eggs. Sheep wool follows the 1.21.5 climate rule (mostly white, brown in warm biomes, black in cold ones); dyed sheep show the undercoat when sheared. Lambs mix their parents' dyes like the crafting recipes do.
  - Spawning follows `NaturalSpawner`: monsters every tick and animals every 400 ticks, in ticking chunks within 128 blocks of the player, a random height from the bottom to just above the surface, three packs of up to four, 24 blocks clear of the player and the world spawn, the light rules (sky light below a random 0..31, no block light, darkness at most a random 0..7) and the caps (70 and 10 per 289 chunks). Animals fill newly generated chunks (vanilla's 10% chance, from a per-chunk random); such chunks are saved with a mark so they never get them twice. Biome spawn lists are vanilla's, limited to the eight mobs. Regional difficulty ignores the time players spent in a chunk (always 0), so on a young Normal world mobs get no random armor, as in vanilla.
  - Despawning: at once beyond 128 blocks, now and then beyond 32 when idle for 30 seconds (faster in the light), all monsters at once on Peaceful; animals stay.
  - Combat: the player's attack (the cooldown scaling, critical hits, sprint knockback, sword sweeps with their particles, damage indicators, weapon wear, hunger), mob melee (zombies 3, spiders 2, skeletons 2 with their weapon's bonus), knockback from where the hit came from, difficulty scaling of mob damage, skeleton bows with vanilla's aim and spread, creeper fuses (3 blocks, 7 to give up) and blasts (power 3, 6 when charged by lightning; drops survive one time in the power, and with `mobGriefing` off nothing breaks and items aren't destroyed). Shields don't block (render only), and there are no enchantments. Arrows fly, stick and shake as in vanilla; they don't catch fire, don't stick in bodies, and their pickup doesn't fly to the player on screen. Tipped and spectral arrows shoot as plain arrows.
  - The bow: drawn with arrows in the inventory (or in creative), shot on release at vanilla's power (critical at full draw), worn by one; drawing narrows the view (FOV effects). Holding a bow while using it shows its pull in both views.
  - Animals: fed to breed (hearts, babies after 3 seconds together, 5 minutes of rest, 1-7 experience), feeding babies speeds their growth by a tenth, babies grow up in 20 minutes and follow adults. Sheep are sheared (1-3 wool) and dyed, and regrow by eating grass (which turns the grass to dirt with `mobGriefing`). Chickens lay an egg every 5-10 minutes and flutter down. Cows give milk. Spawn eggs spawn on the clicked face, or make a baby when used on a mob of their kind (zombies included).
  - The undead burn in daylight under the open sky unless wet or wearing a helmet (which wears instead); skeletons avoid the sun by day and look for shade when burning. Burning mobs and players are drawn in vanilla's flames. Mobs with `NoAI` (`/summon`) keep physics, burning and their own ticks, as in vanilla.
  - Death: mobs fall over red for a second and vanish in smoke; loot from vanilla's tables (`loot` in `data/entities.json`: cooked when burning, the player-kill drops, music discs from creepers a skeleton killed), worn and held items at 8.5% after a player's hit, experience after a player's hit (animals 1-3, monsters 5 plus their equipment, baby zombies 12). Death messages name the killer ("Player was slain by Zombie").
